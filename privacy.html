Day Recap

Privacy Policy

Effective October 30, 2026 (updated for Day Recap 1.5: automatic journal, places and trips, voice, letters, habits and Face ID)

Day Recap is a private daily journal. This policy explains what the app stores, where it stays, and how Apple Health is used. The short version: everything you enter stays on your iPhone. We don't run servers for the app, we don't have accounts, and we don't collect, see, sell or share your data.

What Day Recap stores

When you log a day, Day Recap saves the following on your device:

  • Your day rating (1–10)
  • Optional mood, energy, productivity and diet scores (1–10)
  • Optional sleep hours and whether you went to the gym
  • If you connect Apple Health, each logged day's step count
  • Your diary text, and answers to guided reflections
  • Voice recordings you make, and the text written from them
  • Letters to your future self (text, voice or video), sealed until the date you choose
  • Habits you choose to keep, their reminder time and the days you ticked them
  • If you turn on the automatic journal: each day's weather, number of calendar events, minutes walking and in a vehicle, time in daylight, resting heart rate, and the places you visited
  • The names of people you choose to add, and which days they were part of
  • Whether a day is starred, and your note about what made it special
  • Photos you attach to a day
  • Factors you choose to track yourself (for example "Alcohol" or "Meditation") and your answers to them
  • If you connect Screen Time, your daily screen time as a band (for example "2h–2h 30m")
  • Your app preferences, such as your good-day threshold, week start, reminder time and appearance

The calendar, statistics and insights are calculated on your device from this information.

Where your data is kept

All of this data is stored in the app's private storage on your iPhone. Day Recap does not upload it anywhere. There is no Day Recap account, no cloud sync, and no server that receives your journal.

If you use iCloud Backup or back up your iPhone to a computer, iOS may include Day Recap's data in that device backup, as it does for most apps. Those backups are created and protected by Apple under your Apple Account settings; we have no access to them.

Photos

You can attach photos to a day using Apple's photo picker or the camera. If you allow access to your photo library, Day Recap also shows the photos you took on a given day so you can add them in one tap; it only reads them on your iPhone and never uploads them. You can choose limited access or no access in iOS. Each photo is resized and saved inside the app on your device. Location and other metadata embedded in the original photo are not kept. Removing a photo from a day deletes it from the app.

Automatic journal

The automatic journal is optional. Each source has its own switch (during onboarding or in Settings › Automatic journal), and iOS asks for permission only when you turn it on.

  • Calendar: Day Recap counts the events on the calendars you choose each day. Event titles, attendees and details are not stored.
  • Places and trips: with location access, iOS tells Day Recap about the places you visit. Day Recap stores them on your iPhone to show each day's places on a map and to turn stays 50 km or more from home into trip stories. Your location is never sent to us or anyone else.
  • Weather: to get the day's weather, your approximate location is sent to Apple's WeatherKit service, under Apple's privacy policy. Only the weather result is stored.
  • Walking and travel: with Motion & Fitness access, Day Recap reads how many minutes you walked or travelled each day.

Maps are drawn with Apple's MapKit, which loads map images from Apple. You can turn any source off at any time, in Day Recap's Settings or in the iPhone Settings app.

Voice, letters and reflections

When you record your voice, the recording is saved on your iPhone with the day. Speech is turned into text on the device; nothing is sent to Apple or anyone else for transcription. Letters to your future self, including any video, stay on your iPhone and stay sealed until the date you chose.

Face ID

If you turn on the lock in Settings, Day Recap uses Face ID or your passcode to unlock the app. This is handled by iOS: Day Recap never sees or stores your face data.

Screen Time

Connecting Screen Time is optional and is done in Day Recap's Settings. It uses Apple's Screen Time framework, which asks for your permission. You choose what counts, ideally "All Apps & Categories". iOS then lets Day Recap know each time that day's usage passes a mark such as 30 minutes or 2 hours, and Day Recap saves the band reached for each day.

Day Recap never learns which apps or websites you used, or for how long each one was open. Apple keeps that private. The daily band is stored on your iPhone with the rest of your journal and is never sent anywhere. You can disconnect at any time in Day Recap's Settings, or revoke access in the iPhone Settings app under Screen Time.

Widgets

If you add Day Recap widgets, the app keeps a small summary of recent ratings and starred days in a storage area on your iPhone that only Day Recap and its widgets can read. Ratings you tap in a widget are stored there briefly until the app saves them into your journal. Diary text, people, photos and Health data are not included in the widget summary.

Apple Health

Connecting Apple Health is optional. Day Recap only asks for permission when you tap Connect Apple Health (during onboarding or in Settings).

  • What it reads: sleep analysis, workouts, step count, time in daylight, resting heart rate and State of Mind. Nothing else.
  • Why: to fill in the sleep hours and gym fields of your daily entry. A recorded workout marks the day as a gym day. You can change or clear these values at any time. Your daily step total is saved with each day you log so insights can compare more and less active days; it never leaves your iPhone.
  • What it writes: nothing. Day Recap never writes to Apple Health.
  • Where it goes: the values you keep are saved in your journal on your device, like everything else. Health data is never sent off your device, never shared with anyone, and never used for advertising or marketing.

If you decline, Day Recap works fully and you can enter sleep and workouts yourself. You can change access at any time in the iPhone Settings app under Health › Data Access & Devices › Day Recap.

Notifications

If you turn on the evening reminder or a habit reminder, Day Recap asks for permission to send notifications. Reminders are scheduled on your device by iOS and contain only a short prompt (for a habit, its name), never your journal content. You can turn it off in Day Recap's Settings or in the iPhone Settings app.

Analytics, advertising and tracking

Day Recap contains no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or tracking software. Its only network requests go to Apple: WeatherKit for the day's weather and MapKit for map images, if you use them. We do not run any server that receives your data. We do not track you across apps or websites.

Day Recap may occasionally show Apple's standard App Store rating prompt. That prompt is provided and handled by Apple.

Sharing

We don't share your data with anyone, because we never receive it.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on your device until you remove it. You can edit any day at any time. Removing a person in Settings hides them from your lists, while days you already logged keep their history. Deleting Day Recap from your iPhone permanently deletes all of its data from the device. Data inside an existing iCloud or computer backup is managed by Apple and removed according to your backup settings.

Children

Day Recap is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If Day Recap's handling of data changes, for example if optional cloud sync is added in the future, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the effective date above will change.
